The Group Stage

SCHEDULE

Everyone plays everyone

The group stage runs over 5 matchdays, and all six teams play on every one of them. You will face each of the other five teams exactly once, which makes 15 matches in total across the group.

Every group match is a Best of 3: the first team to win two rounds takes the match, so a match ends 2–0 or 2–1.

In KOTAO a match is a series between two teams, and a round is one game inside it. You win rounds to win the match.

NO DRAWS

A round always has a winner

There are no draws in KOTAO. If a round finishes level, it goes straight to sudden death: no time limit, first goal wins.

If that mini-match somehow ends goalless too, you replay it again, and again, for as long as it takes. Only the decisive scoreline is recorded — so a round is never written down as 2–2.

STANDINGS

How you are ranked

Your position comes down to matches won first. When two or more teams end up level on that, these separate them, in this exact order:

  1. Round difference — rounds you won minus rounds you lost
  2. Rounds won
  3. Goal difference — goals scored minus goals conceded
  4. Goals scored
  5. Head-to-head — if you beat the team you are level with, you finish above them

Since no match can end in a draw, head-to-head always settles a tie between two teams. Your name never decides anything: there is no alphabetical tiebreak.

With three or more teams level, head-to-head counts the wins each of them took off the others, then is applied again to any teams still level. The only tie it cannot break is a perfect circle — A beat B, B beat C, C beat A. Those teams play a tiebreak match, and the playoffs wait for its result.

The Playoffs

QUALIFICATION

Nobody goes home after the group stage

All six teams reach the playoffs. Where you finish in the group only decides which door you come in through:

  • 1st – 4th start in the Upper Bracket
  • 5th – 6th start in the Lower Bracket

Finishing high is worth real protection, not just pride — but finishing 5th or 6th is not an elimination.

DOUBLE ELIMINATION

A top-four finish buys you a second chance

Lose in the Upper Bracket and you drop into the Lower Bracket instead of being knocked out. Your run continues.

Lose in the Lower Bracket and the tournament is over for you. Teams that finish 5th or 6th start down there, so for them every playoff match is win-or-go-home.

The Grand Final is the exception: it is a single match with no bracket reset, so whoever loses it is done — even a team that arrived undefeated.

BO5 means first to three rounds; BO7 means first to four. A series stops the moment one team gets there.

FINAL PLACINGS

Where every team finishes

1st — Champion Winner of the Grand Final
2nd Loser of the Grand Final
3rd Loser of the Lower Bracket Final
4th Loser of the Lower Bracket Semifinal
5th – 6th Losers of the two Lower Bracket Quarterfinals
GRAND FINAL

No bracket reset

The Grand Final is a single Best of 7. Whoever wins it is the champion — there is no second series for the Upper Bracket team, even though they arrived undefeated.

This is a deliberate choice. A bracket reset would add a second Best of 7 — up to seven more rounds for two teams who may already have played seven. One final, one champion.

Conduct

FAIR PLAY

No exploiting

Exploiting means a disqualification for that fixture. Do not “F to tp”, and do not velostrap H.

Everyone is expected to have read this page before they play. There will be no excuses on the day.

RESULTS

Where the scores come from

Admins record every round in the official tracker, and this site is generated straight from it — standings, tiebreakers and the bracket are all recalculated from the raw scores, never typed in by hand.

The footer of every page shows when the results were last updated. If a match you played is not showing yet, it simply has not been published — give it a moment.
